## PASS · ok_ssb_vs_tbill_explain

**Q:** How do Singapore Savings Bonds and T-bills differ?

**Sections sent:** none · **Mode:** knowledge

- Singapore Savings Bonds (SSBs) are issued by the Singapore Government and are designed for individual investors, offering a step-up interest rate that increases the longer the bond is held. They can be redeemed in any month without penalty and are fully backed by the government, ensuring no capital loss.

- SSBs have a minimum investment of $500 and an individual holding limit of $200,000. They pay interest every six months, and the interest earned is exempt from tax.

- Treasury Bills (T-bills) are also government securities but are short-duration instruments with maturities of 6 months or 1 year. They are auctioned regularly and are typically purchased at a discount, redeemable at face value upon maturity.

- Unlike SSBs, T-bills cannot be redeemed early and must be held to maturity. They do not pay periodic interest (coupons) and are less flexible for investors needing immediate access to funds.

- SSBs are available only to Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents, while T-bills can be purchased with cash or CPF Ordinary Account funds.
