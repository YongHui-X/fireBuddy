## PASS · ssb_or_tbill_my_money

**Q:** Should I put my savings in Singapore Savings Bonds or T-bills?

**Sections sent:** cashflow · **Mode:** hybrid

- Singapore Savings Bonds (SSBs) are backed by the Singapore Government, making them a safe investment with no capital loss.
- They offer a step-up interest rate that increases the longer you hold the bond, and you can redeem them any month without penalty.
- The minimum investment for SSBs is $500, with a maximum holding limit of $200,000.
- SSBs are suitable for individuals aged 18 and above and are not available to foreigners.

- Treasury Bills (T-bills) are short-duration government securities with maturities of 6 months or 1 year.
- T-bills are auctioned regularly and must be held to maturity; they cannot be redeemed early.
- They are typically issued at a discount and redeemed at face value, and can be purchased with cash or CPF Ordinary Account funds.

## PASS · cpf_topup_or_sti

**Q:** Should I top up my CPF or buy the STI ETF instead?

**Sections sent:** none · **Mode:** knowledge

- Topping up your CPF can earn you a guaranteed return of 4% per annum for Special Account (SA) top-ups, and you can receive income tax relief up to $8,000 per year for self-top-ups and another $8,000 for top-ups to family members.
- However, once money is in CPF, it is illiquid until retirement age, which may create cash flow issues for early retirees.
- The Ordinary Account (OA) earns only 2.5%, and voluntary top-ups to the OA are less common.
- Investing in the STI ETF through the CPF Investment Scheme (CPFIS) allows you to use OA funds, and dividends from the ETF are tax-free for Singapore individual investors.
- The expense ratios for both Nikko AM STI ETF and SPDR STI ETF are approximately 0.3%.
- Historical returns of the STI ETF have lagged behind global indices, so many investors use it primarily for local diversification or as a CPF investment.
