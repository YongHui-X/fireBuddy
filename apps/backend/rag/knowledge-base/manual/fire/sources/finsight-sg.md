# FinSight SG on a Singapore FIRE number

## Source role

This is an April 2026 third party article, not an official CPF guide. It argues that a Singapore FIRE plan should reflect local spending, CPF LIFE, housing, tax, healthcare, and the long bridge faced by early retirees. Its strongest contribution is the two phase framing. Several broad claims require qualification before use in a financial knowledge base.

## Proposed inputs and formula

The article starts from the classic target:

`FIRE number = annual retirement spending / withdrawal rate`

It asks the reader to estimate actual retirement spending and remove current costs expected to end, such as work related spending or a mortgage that will be paid off. It then proposes subtracting estimated CPF LIFE income from spending and applying the withdrawal rate to the remaining annual gap.

The article discusses a 4 percent rate for later retirement and suggests 3.5 percent as a more conservative third party assumption for early retirement. These are planning choices, not guaranteed safe rates and not official Singapore rules.

## CPF bridge concept

The useful correction appears later in the article: someone retiring before CPF LIFE starts needs two phases.

* Before CPF LIFE, the liquid portfolio funds the full spending gap after any genuinely available income.
* From the chosen CPF LIFE payout age, the portfolio funds spending after the actual payout for that phase.

This means the article's simple gap divided by withdrawal rate shortcut should not be applied to every retirement year. A dated cash flow or separate bridge target is needed when retirement begins before the income stream.

## Housing and liquidity

The article recommends treating a paid off HDB home as a safety net rather than an investable FIRE portfolio. The home may lower future housing costs and may later be monetised by a deliberate action, but paper equity cannot fund monthly withdrawals by itself.

This is an important FireBuddy rule: do not count property equity as liquid assets by default. Include only explicit proceeds or income from a user selected sale, right sizing plan, lease monetisation, or rental scenario.

## Outputs and presentation ideas

The article's implied outputs are current spending, adjusted retirement spending, expected CPF LIFE income, portfolio funded gap, FIRE target, and a separate early retirement bridge. It promotes a free FinSight SG calculator that reportedly models CPF separately, allows a custom retirement age, uses two phase cash flows, requires no login, and keeps app data on the device.

The article itself does not publish sufficient formula detail to independently reproduce all calculator results. Its examples and lifestyle ranges are publisher opinions rather than statistical benchmarks suitable for defaults.

## Official fact checking

CPF Board confirms that CPF LIFE provides lifelong monthly payouts and may start from age 65 to 70. It does not support describing every CPF LIFE payout as inflation linked. Only the Escalating Plan grows by 2 percent each year. Standard payouts are steady, while Basic payouts can decline as balances fall.

For members turning 55 in 2026, official CPF Board reference sums are S$110,200 BRS, S$220,400 FRS, and S$440,800 ERS. Example monthly payouts depend on the member profile and plan. They must not be applied as a universal future forecast.

CPF is also not simply available as liquid retirement cash before age 55. From age 55, withdrawal eligibility depends on the applicable retirement sum and property conditions. CPFIS allows eligible CPF savings to be invested under scheme rules, but an investment facility is not the same as unrestricted cash access.

The article's broad statements about typical retirement spending, healthcare coverage, housing status, investment returns, and tax advantages should remain attributed to FinSight SG. They should not become FireBuddy defaults without official or statistical evidence.

## FireBuddy takeaway

Keep the transparent target for v1. Later, add CPF as a separate dated income stream with a visible bridge period. Show liquid portfolio, CPF retirement income, and home equity separately. Never reduce all three to one headline net worth number.
