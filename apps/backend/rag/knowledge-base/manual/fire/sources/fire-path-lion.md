# FIRE Path Lion on itemised retirement spending

## Source role

This personal Singapore FIRE article was originally published in 2019 and later updated. It documents a change from a rough monthly spending estimate to an itemised retirement budget. It is a worked personal example, not a calculator methodology or a statement of what another household should spend.

## Inputs and arithmetic

The author originally estimated S$6,000 monthly spending and used a personal 3.33 percent withdrawal rate. The resulting target was about S$2.16 million.

The revised budget itemised parents' allowance, support for two children, mortgage or rent, utilities, transport, food, phone, several insurance premiums, and travel. The published annual total is about S$56,440, or roughly S$4,700 per month.

The implied calculation is:

`FIRE target = annual retirement expenses / 0.0333`

Using the itemised total gives about S$1.695 million. The author then chose a S$5,000 monthly planning budget, including a buffer, producing about S$1.802 million. The article estimates that this would shorten the personal accumulation period compared with the original rough target.

## Outputs and useful concepts

The source's output is a revised FIRE target and a change in estimated years to reach it. Its durable lesson is that spending is the primary driver of a withdrawal rate target. A rough rounded lifestyle number can hide a large buffer or omit an important obligation.

FireBuddy can improve this workflow by deriving an annual retirement spending baseline from completed expense transactions, showing the covered period and categories, and allowing a clearly labelled manual override. Users should then add or remove retirement specific items consciously rather than accepting an unexplained default.

The article also supports category FI layers. Each annual category amount can be divided by the same withdrawal rate to show its contribution to the total target. A buffer should be an explicit category or assumption, not hidden rounding.

## Limitations and fact checking

The expense amounts are one person's dated plan. Family support, insurance, housing, travel, and inflation can differ substantially. The 3.33 percent rate is the author's conservative choice, not a guaranteed safe rate.

The formula produces a target but does not simulate portfolio drawdown, return sequence risk, taxes, fees, changes in expenses, healthcare shocks, CPF LIFE timing, SRS access, or property liquidity. It also assumes that all annual expenses continue under the same withdrawal framework.

No CPF or SRS rule from this article should be treated as official. A later Singapore cash flow model must use current CPF Board and IRAS sources and must preserve the liquid bridge before restricted or age based income becomes available.

## FireBuddy takeaway

Use actual transaction history as evidence, then let the user edit retirement assumptions. Present both the baseline and override, the numerical target difference, and which categories caused it. This makes the calculation reproducible without pretending that current spending is a perfect forecast.
