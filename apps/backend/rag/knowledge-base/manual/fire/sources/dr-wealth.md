# Dr Wealth FIRE guide

## Source role

Dr Wealth provides an introductory explanation of FIRE rather than an interactive calculator. It connects the 25 times rule with aggressive saving, increased income, and long term investing. The article mixes general concepts, older personal examples, and publisher opinion, so dated CPF statements require care.

## Inputs and core formula

The core input is annual spending. The article describes the 25 times guideline:

`FIRE target = annual expenses x 25`

This is algebraically the same as:

`FIRE target = annual expenses / 0.04`

The source treats this as a starting estimate based on a 4 percent initial withdrawal guideline. It then encourages readers to review spending, savings rate, income, and investments to estimate how quickly the target might be reached.

## Outputs and concepts

The main output is a personal FIRE number. The article does not provide a reproducible projection engine, simulation, chart, scenario comparison, or stated privacy model because it is educational content rather than a calculator.

Its useful conceptual sequence is:

1. Understand how lifestyle spending determines the target.
2. Reduce spending in a sustainable way and increase the savings rate.
3. Increase income where practical.
4. Invest consistently over a long horizon while accepting that returns involve risk.

The article also reframes purchases in terms of work time. This can support educational content, but it should not become an authoritative FireBuddy calculation unless income, working hours, and tax treatment are explicitly defined.

## Limitations

The 25 times rule is a planning shortcut, not a retirement drawdown guarantee. It does not by itself specify retirement length, asset mix, fees, taxes, inflation adjustment mechanics, future cash flows, CPF, SRS, healthcare, or sequence risk. A user who retires early may have a much longer horizon than the common 30 year studies associated with the 4 percent guideline.

The article includes personal and historical examples with figures that may no longer be current. Its statements about CPF accessibility, contribution rates, and returns should remain attributed opinion rather than current official rules.

## Official CPF qualification

CPF balances are separated into accounts with different uses, rates, and access rules. It is inaccurate to compress the system into one universally available balance earning one fixed return.

CPF Board states that members can generally apply for retirement withdrawals from age 55, subject to retirement sum and property conditions. CPF LIFE payouts may begin from age 65 to 70 and then continue for life. The payout depends on RA balance, start age, interest assumptions, and mortality. These official mechanics are more precise than the article's broad language.

## FireBuddy takeaway

The 25 times rule is appropriate for a simple, explainable v1 when the withdrawal rate is editable and the assumptions are visible. FireBuddy should calculate the number deterministically, show the transaction based spending source, and explain that a target is not proof that a specific withdrawal path will survive.
